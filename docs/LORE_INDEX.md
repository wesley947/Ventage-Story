# The Great Silence — Lore Index and Continuity Notes

**Last updated:** 2026-11-08  
**Status:** Structured story outline, not yet implemented as game assets. All proposed entry titles/characters are placeholders. Public mod title is undecided.

## Tone and purpose
The ancient writings belong in the atmospheric world of Vintage Story. They should be **recognizably about the loss and eventual return of flatulence**, with lightly veiled but unmistakable puns and physiological references. Serious documents discussing ridiculous matters are funnier than opaque mysteries or overt modern meme jokes. Mix short notes and longer scrolls/books, and distinct voices: aristocrats, physicians, refugees, dissenters, surface botanists and villagers. Players may discover entries out of chronological order; each should work alone but connect to one larger story.

Lore **must be represented in the initial public release** (at least some collectible writings), not pushed entirely to a sequel.

## Story chronology (fictional expansion, NOT confirmed vanilla canon)

### A. The Age of Refinement — aristocratic medicine
Before the Rot/catastrophe, upper society considered uncontrolled flatulence vulgar and undignified, not frightening or inherently dangerous. Wealthy patrons commissioned physicians to suppress intestinal gas. The procedure became a fashionable status symbol; possible class prejudice against the 'commoner's wind.'

### B. The Subterranean Mandate — plague/Rot and selective refuge
As the old world's crisis forced flight underground, space, privacy and ventilation were scarce. Authorities mandated the formerly elite treatment for fortunate people granted access to subterranean refuges. It also became a measure of enforced decorum and control. Restricted cave diets helped maintain its effectiveness. Avoid suggesting every human survived only underground or received this treatment.

### C. The Great Silence — loss of mirth
The treatment succeeded in suppressing normal bodily emissions across these populations; after generations, many had no experience or even suitable vocabulary for farts. Some observers lamented the disappearance of an ancient source of surprise and innocent laughter. A responsible physician questioned whether eliminating embarrassment had cost humanity a piece of its spirit.

### D. The Physician's Prediction
An elder physician's reflective writing anticipates that surface foods and the open air might eventually restore natural digestive phenomena: 'the winds of change' might bring mirth back. They may hope the treatment eventually weakens instead of considering that a failure.

### E. The First Harvests — return of varied diets
A particular survivor community resettling the surface encounters forgotten plants, grains, fruits, vegetables, medicinal herbs and poisonous species. Some foods had not been eaten for generations. The old suppression/microbiome adaptation weakens as diets expand.

### F. Fear, Investigation and First Laughter
Early settlers initially fear unfamiliar lower abdominal sensations and noises, suspect illness, poison, the Rot or something in their environment. A healer discovers it is a benign, diet-related physiological phenomenon. The realization that these noises are harmless becomes a communal moment of relief and laughter; a physician may inadvertently demonstrate the diagnosis. Eventually people recover an ancient name (or invent new humorous names) for 'passing wind.'

### G. Present-day rediscovery
Surviving written fragments and perhaps a resonator recording help connect the strange current bodily phenomenon to old-world history. Different accounts may disagree in plausible ways; some ambiguity is welcome, but the core causal chain must be internally coherent.

## Canon checks before publishing
- Vintage Story's Seraphs are **not automatically ordinary cave-descended humans**; they are tied to ancient temporal displacement and memory loss. Survivor communities and Seraphs may have different knowledge gaps.
- Tobias and surviving communities passed on some agriculture/building/metallurgy knowledge; do not claim all surface food knowledge was universally lost.
- Lore such as *The Morning* evokes coming into a green world, but the exact historic chronology of underground shelter and return is not fully established here. Compare actual 1.22 texts before placing dates or asserting universal cave life.
- Our gas-suppression story, treatment mandate, invented physician, and loss-of-laughter theory are mod fiction—not Vanilla-confirmed facts.
- Use deliberately vague or local-community dates if necessary to avoid contradictions.

## Canon research update — shelter diets are incomplete records (2026-11-08)

**Important correction, from user and canon review:** We **don't know the regular diet of subterranean refuges**, nor exactly how cheesemaking, dairy herds or animal-husbandry skills were preserved/reintroduced afterward. The user's examples of grain stores, salted meat and peanuts are hypotheses, **not facts to put in a narrator's objective voice**.

Existing [Vintage Story Quartermaster's Ledger, Part 1](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Lore_book) reports **dried wolf and rabbit meat**, captured small game and near-starvation in one shelter; this is the strongest concrete dietary evidence found, **but it doesn't cover all underground communities**. [Lore guide](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Guide:Lore_(spoiler_lite)) says Tobias passed on farming/building/metallurgy knowledge after resurfacing, without specifying cheese-making instruction. [Other canonical lore passages](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Lore_book) recount dairy cows before the catastrophe; those don't prove fresh dairy in underground refuges.

**Seraph ancestry:** Tobias identifies Seraphs as transformed humans lost beyond time and later returned, so **do not represent modern Seraphs as a multigenerational cave-born population**. The fictitious Great Silence treatment and restricted diets can instead concern a documented/local *human refugee community*, with later Seraphs experiencing dairy-specific digestive effects for an uncertain reason. Proposed scientists' testimony/rumors must be labeled as tentative in-world theory, not authoritative canon. No lore claim that lack of milk **causes loss of genetically determined lactase persistence**; distinct possible food sensitivity/altered biology is fictional.

**Narrative opportunity:** A torn food ledger mentioning preserved meat, a disputed note about a lost milk herd, or a physician arguing about why milk now causes 'disturbances' could fit the chronology without declaring what all caves ate. Preserve original game's mystery.

## New lore thread — the dairyless lower chambers (2026-11-08)

**User's proposed in-world premise:** Seraphs and/or their ancestral refuge populations went without animal milk and dairy products while sheltered underground. After the return to the surface, encounters with **milk and cheese** cause unusually vigorous intestinal gas, connected in an old physician's writings to lost or reduced lactase function/tolerance. This is a **fictional extension** of Great Silence lore, **not verified vanilla canon**; avoid asserting all Seraphs are literal cave-descended humans (see 'Canon checks' above). The refuge story might apply specifically to one settlement/population and present day observers may misgeneralize it.

**Story beat:** A physician notes that an austere cave ration lacked milk, and that some of the first surface settlers react poorly to new dairy farming. They mistake the 'return of wind' for a new plague or disturbance of the Rot before recognizing a digestive phenomenon. A later healer distinguishes milk-caused distress from cabbage and legumes, and notes eggs cause a *different* problem entirely. Narrate as solemn academic observation with an unmistakably funny conclusion, fitting GS-03, GS-05 and GS-06. Potential additional discoverable field note: **'On the Consequences of Milk'** (working title, optional). No text/loot generated yet.

**Science/gameplay guardrails:** Eggs **contain no lactose**, and aged cheeses tend to contain less lactose than milk; eggs can still be high on the **mod's stylized gas impact list** through independent Seraph-specific sensitivity and/or later sulfur-odor profile. Never claim egg gas proves lactose intolerance or that aging preserves equal lactose. Treat dairy and eggs as distinct ingredient categories even in scrambled eggs with cheese or cheese pie. Sources: [NIDDK lactose intolerance diet](https://www.niddk.nih.gov/health-information/digestive-diseases/lactose-intolerance/eating-diet-nutrition) and [VS Dairy](https://wiki.vintagestory.at/Dairy). The user explicitly wants milk, cheese, eggs, cabbage and legumes notable in v1 fermentation/gas profiles, but exact gas multipliers not yet selected.

## Proposed discovery pacing: vanilla lore first (2026-11-08)

**New design request — not yet implemented or numerically finalized.** The mod should preserve the player's opportunity to collect and enjoy Vintage Story's own lore **before** Ventage Story's Great Silence writings become common. The more of the vanilla journal a player has discovered, the higher the chance that *additional* Ventage scrolls/books appear in appropriate ruins, cracked vessels or chests. The mod's later lore discovery should feel like an expansion of an established world story, not compete with it.

**Strong preference:** DO NOT reduce vanilla lore weights, replace a vanilla book that would have dropped, or take exclusive loot slots. Use a separate rare bonus roll when feasible, then boost only that *additional* roll according to vanilla journal progress. Keep our texts discoverable but extremely rare early; ensure chances are bounded and configurable. Determine concrete values from playtesting. Avoid guaranteeing a Ventage book on every encounter even at 100% vanilla completion.

**Journal lookup:** Vanilla's `ModJournal.DidDiscoverLore(playerUid, loreCode, chapterIndex)` reportedly allows querying whether a *particular player* knows a particular chapter (the 1.20 changelog confirms the method was reintroduced). To derive percentage, enumerate **eligible unique vanilla lore chapters from the current world's content**, then calculate discovered / eligible. Do not include our own/modded lore in the denominator, and don't count inaccessible story-specific chapters as required if the player has not reached/activated that story content. Confirm eligibility and journal iteration APIs from installed 1.22 sources. Compute lazily at relevant loot events or cache/refresh on journal changes; do not scan every tick.

**Loot-hook limitation:** Vanilla `BlockLootVessel.GetDrops(world, pos, byPlayer,...)` and `LootList.GenerateLoot(world, forPlayer)` receive the breaking player, a promising place for per-player bonus logic. However, its `LootItem.GetItemStack` creates a new item stack from an item code without embedded per-stack lore title/text, so a straightforward JSON addition alone might not implement our self-contained written vanilla items; investigate a safe narrow hook/wrapper. Some chest inventories are generated at worldgen or stored ahead of opening, when no player's journal is available. Do not assume all chests roll when opened. To support progress-aware chests, we may need a verified on-first-interaction hook, separate loot reward, or leave pre-generated chest chances static; do not silently reroll/preempt existing contents.

**Multiplayer:** Progress is **per player**, not universal. An event with a clearly identified breaker/opener can use that person's journal; a shared chest whose contents already exist should remain stable and shared (no conflicting per-player rerolls). New bonus books go into world inventory as ordinary readable **vanilla** item stacks with embedded title/text, per the separate uninstall-safety investigation.

**Duplicate handling (correction, 2026-11-08):** **Do not implement blanket duplicate prevention or automatic preference for unread Ventage entries.** Players legitimately want repeat copies to fill bookshelves, trade, and extend collection time. Ordinary world lore demonstrably permits repeat discoveries; the Resonance Archives' player-aware selection is a special case, not proven to be the standard world-drop behavior. Treat a repeated item as a valid collectible; the journal simply should not grant a second *new* discovery for a chapter already read. Keep repeat frequency subject to optional future balancing, not an automatic anti-duplicate rule. Avoid revealing undiscovered lore titles in diagnostics without developer mode.

**Suggested test sequence:** Generate a small known vanilla lore catalog, query progress for a test player at 0%, some entries read, and near/full completion; measure a synthetic bonus roll's rates without modifying vanilla drops. Test a cracked vessel broken by each of two players, an unopened generated chest, and a chest stocked before the mod was installed. Verify add-only drops and no world corruption when the mod is removed. Examine actual in-game journal and loot tables before committing an implementation strategy.

## Proposed lore content workflow
1. Research vanilla writings for length, grammar, perspective and consistency, without reproducing long copyrighted passages.
2. Write canonical chronology and character glossary here, maintaining a single internal timeline.
3. Draft each short/long entry in its own text or JSON asset while keeping an ID/title/arc/voice/status index.
4. Verify actual Vintage Story 1.22 lore registration, journal chapters, cracked vessels/chests/ruin loot, books and scrolls. Do not claim equal effort without testing.
5. Playtest discoverability and out-of-order reading; tune rarity.
6. Document changes and update roadmap; do not treat separate resonator/tapestry ideas as blockers for a first playable release.
